# Milestone 06.01 — Movement, traversal and interaction audit

Audited integrated main `6d125eebd0c16062a4d2ea49e2b4348e48c23a9a` (PR #98), 2026-11-26. [Editable architecture diagrams](movement-06-step01-architecture.md) · [Reproduction commands and evidence](evidence/movement-06/README.md).

## Executive summary

Keep the hybrid grid and continuous geometry, the single canonical stop per tile, the shared action service and the tactical/presentation boundary. The inspected workflows mostly honor those contracts. A new authored movement system would duplicate the architecture already handling support, narrow fits, occupancy, diagonals, stairs, ramps, ladders, Guard and replay.

Two defects warrant bounded engineering. Lethal Guard fire can release a rescued VIP at the origin of the carrier's move while the committed receipt stops at a later cell. Separately, a supported partial tile can be rejected when the best obstacle-clear point has insufficient floor under it even though another point works. Both were reproduced on the audited commit. The Depot also demonstrates a movement feedback gap: a blocked click and hover produce no explanation. Ordinary ground routing and most vertical authority checks passed. The focused integration run remains **RED** due to the inherited MIRA ladder descent visual failure.

**Recommended next assignment:** correct checkpoint-aware passenger release during lethal reactive movement, with replay coverage. Move that bounded part of 06.10 forward. Next validate and repair support-aware candidate selection in 06.02. Start door/window implementation only if the owner approves a specific interaction and authoring contract.

## Current movement lifecycle and authority

1. **Map compilation.** Authored ground, transforms, solids, elevations and explicit links become `MapData`. `SurfacePlacement` attempts one within-cell stop. `SurfaceNavigation` checks support, physical obstacle clearance and swept edges. `MapGraphBuilder` builds the graph from canonical points and traversal links. Static obstacles and live unit occupancy are separate inputs.
2. **Discovery.** The player selects an actor and a floor. Reach highlights come from the move inventory. Targeted hover returns a path and the actual world point used by the ghost. AI uses the same inventory plus specialized occupied routes and mission heuristics. A hypothetical friendly-yield route predicts a possibility. It grants no right to the occupied corridor.
3. **Route and legality.** Reachable-cell enumeration spends the actor's movement budget on weighted graph steps. Nonstoppable cells can contribute to reach. A targeted query obtains a 3D logical route and checks available movement, destination occupancy and current sweep clearance. Cardinal and diagonal paths have different weights, and supported offsets affect the sweep. A legal Move generally costs 1 AP in these modes. The budget and AP are different quantities. The request DTO carries source, actor, destination and expected revision.
4. **Execution.** `TacticalActionService.submit_move` validates actor/lifetime/action window/revision, queries again, opens one transaction and spends AP. Reactions and applicable hazards resolve on ordered logical checkpoints. A lethal reaction can shorten the receipt. Grid occupancy, mission effects and transient action state settle authoritatively. The recorder gets the typed result and its state fingerprint.
5. **Visual handoff.** The result carries committed cells and a route for presentation. Authored links and `TacticalRouteBuilder` expand and refine the visual path. Stairs, ramp segments, ladder entry/exit and the actor-height offset serve presentation. `TacticalActionPresenter` awaits the applicable visual stages and releases the busy window. No visual IK result changes a committed destination, AP, damage or replay.
6. **Replay and lifetimes.** `BattleReplayPlayer` reconstructs Move requests using the replay source and compares receipts and fingerprints. It does not invoke AI planning to recover a route. AI snapshot and coordination revisions protect selected calculations while suspended. The action service still validates the submitted move. Focused stale-request, interruption and replay cases passed. The carrier probe below shows a mismatch inside valid execution, before presentation.

### Lethal Guard during Rescue

Authored prototype battle, Rescue, seed 1, `PlayerUnit1` regular MIRA. The fixture uses a real Rescue to pick up the VIP, clears shot geometry for the control and arms an enemy watcher. The production query confirms the route and the Guard opportunity. A seeded hit at 1 HP makes the reaction lethal. The move requests `(13,4,3)` from `(13,4,1)` through `(13,4,2)`. The receipt correctly truncates to `(13,4,2)`. A direct occupancy query also confirms the VIP could stand at the checkpoint. After the action, the VIP occupies `(13,4,1)`, leaving the checkpoint vacant.

The service calls damage during reaction resolution, which emits defeat before updating `result.target_cell` and the mover's grid position. The defeat callback determines the drop from current registered occupancy. Stationary carrier loss already has a contract and fallback-cell coverage. Reproduce the combination in the product regression suite before changing timing. The presentation-suppressed probe establishes a tactical mismatch regardless of animated path. It has not established the behavior of the fully rendered death/drop handoff or recorded historical replay compatibility for this scenario. ### Support selection at a floor edge

Flat 7×7 map, seed 16, 1 m cells. Target `(3,0,3)`, east support removed at `(4,0,3)`. The box and safety margin push the best fit to `(0.33,0,0)`, clear of the box. Its 0.28 m radius extends into the unsupported neighboring cell. The canonical resolver rejects the tile. The alternative `(0,0,0.43)` is inside the same tile, clears the box, has full support and passes the production sweep from neighboring `(3,0,4)` at world `(0,0,1)`. Restoring the east support makes the original point valid. This rules out lack of any safe approach in the fixture. Candidate generation/ranking currently receives only geometry clearance, and support is checked after the winning fit has been chosen. The fix should evaluate support within bounded ranking or advance to the next qualifying candidate in the same deterministic order. Verify canonical signatures and preserve old compilation/replay behavior when points change. ### UI and rendered traversal

The Depot preview probe feeds the matched positive stop `(26,0,6)` and blocked tank center `(30,0,8)` through the production hover and click handlers. The positive stop produces text, and rejection reports generic `unreachable` at the service. The blocked hover and click both leave empty text and do not alter revision or AP. Occupied targets or insufficient connecting reach can be filtered before a reason is available. The issue is compounded because destination occupancy can collapse to `unreachable` before the later occupied check. Before adding more labels, test the rejection precedence for blocked, occupied, beyond range and wrong viewed floor. [Preview probe](evidence/movement-06/preview_probe.gd), [preview and click](../../systems/battle_controller.gd#L462), [move query gating](../../systems/battle_controller.gd#L331).

F3's translucent green is a map-level walkability visualization, including terrain under actors and nonstoppable transit. The normal selected actor range adds occupancy and movement budget. Fill uses logical X/Z with the appropriate display elevation. The ghost lives on the canonical point. Three layers can be legitimate at one spot. A player should be able to distinguish where an actor may stop, where it may only pass, and why a nearby stop is currently unavailable. Preserve existing overlay priority and cover/Guard indicators. The tactical stair screenshots show the floor bar and Alt+wheel prompt. No manual floor-stack pick, cancellation or stacked overlay confusion was asserted. [Ground tile layers](../../presentation/overlays/ground_tile_layers.gd), [F3 map refresh](../../ui/debug_tools.gd#L776), [mouse floor picking](../../systems/grid/mouse_raycaster.gd).

In the Test Range, regular MIRA ran four real-time Move calls on the authored terrain. Stair ascent used `(6,0,15)` → `(6,3,12)`, ramp descent `(11,3,12)` → `(11,0,15)`, and ladders linked `(12,0,4)` ↔ `(12,3,5)`. A motion-quality sign-off still needs owner review of the sequence at normal speed from both rigs, especially the top landing on descent. The carry pose claims limbs, so there is no dedicated planted stair gait or ladder hand/foot contact while loaded. The passenger stays attached in the tested climbs. The map's ordinary body fit should not be read as checking the passenger's complete silhouette. If carry ladders remain legal, test torso/passenger clearance at a real tight deck and decide on a reasonable visual or authored-content restriction. ## Performance, determinism and ownership risks

The movement timing probe called the exact warmed player move inventory and targeted query. Eight calls warmed each case and 128 samples were collected per query. Timings are synchronous elapsed milliseconds on an AMD Ryzen 5 5500, NVIDIA RTX 5060, with the real-site renderer active. The fixture repeats a nearby target without movement and checks the tactical fingerprint and RNG afterwards. It measures neither the frame rate nor the full AI activation, and its sample maximum is sensitive to OS scheduling.

| Service Depot 17 | 40 | 1.56 / 1.69 | 1.60 / 2.26 |
| Freight Yard 16 | 71 | 3.23 / 3.70 | 3.26 / 3.49 |

Targeted queries rebuild the reachability inventory before pathfinding; the preview cache concerns feedback products, and repeated real hovers have other costs. The real-site comparison timed proposed-origin combat options separately (P95 0.51–3.52 ms). These results identify useful regression cases for feedback changes. They provide no evidence for reopening the pathfinding optimizer. Long searches, invalidation under live topology changes, dense vertical AI routing and frame spikes were not stress-tested here. [Movement timing probe](evidence/movement-06/movement_query_cost_probe.gd).

Ownership is already effective where authority queries are reused and presentation consumes committed results. The main fragility is ordering and dependency boundaries:

- **Commit and callbacks.** The defeat signal crosses service, occupancy and objectives before the service publishes the authoritative checkpoint. A scoped transaction-aware fix could use the actual committed defeat location for release without introducing transient public actor moves at every Guard checkpoint. Keep future ordering choices observable via receipts and fingerprints.
- **Placement and replay.** More accurate candidate selection may change existing canonical positions. Content manifests track geometry and standing-point signatures. Measure per-map signature deltas and choose explicit compatibility for old recordings before changing a default. Keep the compiler's authored/legacy mode boundary.
- **Dynamic state.** Cooperative AI planning protects input snapshots and the current action revision. Static surface/route caches assume immutable compiled geometry except at explicit rebuilds. `SurfaceNavigation.traversal_route` keys quantized positions and profile dimensions and uses a part-count cacheability guard. A future door changing an unchanged-count set of solids must explicitly invalidate geometry indexes, placement/route caches, navigation and the corresponding AI and F3 views. Treat this as a feature prerequisite. No stale-door bug was observed because there is no such transition today.
- **Performance boundary.** Retain PR #94's exact clearance reuse and cooperative budgeting. Prior rendered worst-case improvement was roughly 166–167 ms to 31–32 ms for the carrier case. The existing profile still lists slower noncarrier exit and movement scoring on other sites. Track those under #4, then retest if the geometry and occupancy work materially changes input signatures. [AI architecture and measurements](../ai-architecture/performance.md), [responsiveness report](ai-planning-responsiveness-2026-11-25.md).
