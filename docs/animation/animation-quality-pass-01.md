# Animation quality pass 01

Baseline: local `main` at `20180f7`, with the published Task 14 corrections and ladder stabilization behind it. This installment covers gripping and shared frame timing. The supported ladder exit and full-body weight-transfer experiments remain open.

## Gripping

The right index previously followed the trigger-rest policy on a planted ladder hand, even though the wrist was following environmental contact. In the old modifier, a ladder curl of 0.6 with zero trigger pull gave the right index a curl of 0.21. The other right fingers received 0.6.

The shared hand modifier now distinguishes a wrap from an indexed firearm grasp. An active contact uses the wrap, and the armed rig requests indexed behavior only during a firearm hold. Trigger rest and pull still shape the firing index. A support hand receives the full wrap curl. The comparison uses the same existing final-skeleton ladder fixture for MIRA and Small MIRA, armed and unarmed. Its planted-contact control checks equal index and middle-finger input on both hands. Its firearm control checks the firing index's distinct rest and pull and the normal wrap of the support index.

Hand setup now keeps each palm length independently. With usable digits, missing chains leave wrist orientation and intact digits available, and the rig reports each skipped chain. A rig with no usable digit chains keeps its existing arm-IK fallback. Passenger contacts use their own hand’s palm length. The rifle support wrist still uses the established firing-palm reference. The first trial with independent rifle wrist offsets increased relaxed-to-shot support drift to 4.70 mm against 1.65 mm from an already aimed pose, failing the existing readiness contract. That fit needs a separate calibration before changing it. The current curl stage still assumes a palms-down rest orientation, and contact curl does not test digit penetration against a prop. A future support accessory needs an authored local grip frame and a reach fallback. None of that requires replacing arm IK or introducing a multi-finger solver.

## Frame timing

Tactical, exploration and the workbench pass their existing presentation delta into the same rig method. Weapon carry blending, carry phase and carrier preparation use this delta. Loaning a delta to the passenger filter is limited to one skeleton evaluation per supplied engine frame. During a pause, a later skeleton evaluation can align the attachment without advancing the filter again. Editor refreshes use zero elapsed time.

A fixed-step regression pauses the real workbench, switches to movement, and supplies one 40 ms step. The presenter's elapsed time must advance by 40 ms, and the carry blend by 0.20 at the existing 5/s rate. This catches the old divergence between the requested step and engine delta. The 2 Hz carry motion is retained. Testing a gait-synchronized version requires matched moving and stopped captures to judge visual benefit.

## Ladder support and continuity

Existing visual references include the [side/front/three-quarter armed ladder sheet](../claude/images/ladder55/after-up-armed.png), the [top-out](../claude/images/ladder55/after-topout-unarmed.png), and the [stabilization report](../claude/ladder-stabilization-2026-11-22.md). The current handoff still lacks a convincing transfer to the landing. The legacy solver's nominal deck target is a fixed profile offset from the rung plane and the planner releases it according to reach. The exploration controller places its pawn at `top_z` on completion. The tactical route uses a separate horizontal segment after the vertical lane. These differences matter to support point stability in world space.

A shared top-out needs explicit landing geometry or interaction metadata. The first useful experiment should pass the same kind of landing description from the existing workbench, an authored tactical fixture and the exploration platform. The planner can then check a hand brace and lead-sole contact from measured reach, keep a rung contact until the surface is acquired, and shift the visual pelvis over the planted foot. Missing or unreachable geometry should retain the current exit. Preview the final transfer normal-speed on both bodies with a visible deck before enabling it in production. Check interruption at acquisition and release, then verify the tactical route and replay. A pose fitted to the last rung alone cannot verify this behavior.

Stance-foot drift has not been measured across normal walk, turn and stop. A future trial can sample heel and ball drift in world space before considering a short anchor. Weapon settling and rescue lift/release polish remain experimental. This installment changes no support geometry, tactical resources, route points or animation-event timelines.
