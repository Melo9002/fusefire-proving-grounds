# How FuseFire chooses an action

[Architecture index](README.md) · [Diagrams](diagrams.md) · [Walkthroughs](walkthroughs.md) · [Performance](performance.md)

## From observation to action

1. **Activation.** The battle scene attaches a per-unit `AIController`. The turn manager owns round, active unit, phases, AP refresh and eligibility (including free objective actions). Normal play lets AI drive enemies and allies. The debug simulator can drive players too. The controller admits its own active unit, resets activation-local inspection state, looks up the squad and intent, and starts a coroutine. Replay mode disables AI control. An extraction can select the successor during the old commit. The successor defers planning until the service window closes.
2. **Observation.** Units read live rosters, faction relationships, stats and conditions, positions, occupancy, map surfaces/cover, objectives and squad commitments. There is no perception memory or fog-of-war filter here. A hostile may be known through the roster but unshootable because shared LOS, range, or action rules reject the shot. The planning snapshot is a revision plus a copy of occupancy for stale detection across yields. It does not freeze a complete board.
3. **Mission intent.** `ObjectiveManager.get_mission_intent` tests active faction objectives in order: Rescue, eligible Extract, Reach, Terminal, Protect, Survive, Eliminate. Enemy evacuation uses an enemy extraction goal and a player-side `stop_enemy_evacuation` goal recognized by the interception heuristic. The controller suppresses automated escort extraction while a friendly carrier needs support. A VIP has its own follow-or-wait branch. Protect keeps units near the protected actor. Survive looks for protected separation during the timer and can transition to extraction. Mission contacts and outcomes still belong to the objective system.
4. **Policy.** Difficulty provides decision weights, lapse policy, safe-advance tolerance and the Move→Attack capability gate. Easy uses weaker cover/exposure/finishing weights and can select a near-best legal option more often. Normal can reposition for a shot and has smaller lapses. Hard avoids lapses and links wounded-target value to the probability of landing affordable shots. Commander doctrine modifies declared weights and the safe-advance tolerance per side. Its cached composition is a separate layer. Situational movement profiles multiply the result for a particular movement problem. Objective and Carrier layers can stack. Escort, Defensive, Support, Flanking and Aggressive are contextual stances. None of these grant actual accuracy, damage, AP, SP, or movement.
5. **Candidates.** BattleController's ordinary query adapters obtain legal destinations, targets and actions through the authority service. The movement inventory starts with reachable/stoppable cells and tests occupancy. A targeted move query repeats that inventory, checks the exact final path and its budget, then packages presentation data. A candidate can still fail a squad reservation, second-move exposure test, route relevance filter or final movement validation. Aim, Guard, Shield, rescue, extraction and terminal interaction also have live legality queries. Hypothetical forecasts use combat geometry or occupancy-aware path queries, which are estimates for planning.
6. **Evaluation.** The controller uses precedence across action families and utility within selected comparisons. Target score combines HP-capped expected damage, vulnerability and projected finish probability with threat to allies, VIP and mission value, and the squad's focus adjustment. Position score combines route and direct progress, directional cover, incoming shot exposure, outgoing firing potential, nearby threats, spacing and sometimes flank/cohesion. The controller subtracts a recent-origin penalty, compares a move with holding, and adds bounded urgency after low-value actions. A carrier prefers forward progress but can take a necessary detour. Optional near-best lapses operate on bounded alternatives and the movement lapse excludes mission routes and carriers.
7. **Short planning.** `AIPlanEvaluator` estimates expected damage and finish value within affordable current AP/SP, capped to three AP for these attack comparisons. Aim compares shooting immediately with spending an AP to prepare a better first shot. Move→Attack is considered with a current legal target, no prior move, enough resources, allowed policy and no friendly carrier/VIP condition. It compares predicted shots from legal cells with current shooting value, then checks exposure for up to twelve ranked finalists. It requires a gain of at least 3 or 25% of the current value, whichever is greater. There is no tree of future turns. Route cost to a distant goal and an enemy's estimated next-activation travel are navigation heuristics. After committing the first step, the controller reevaluates.
8. **Commit.** The action service checks battle lifetime, pause, busy window, revision, live actor, resources and action-specific facts. It applies the authorized operation synchronously, publishes its transaction and later releases its window after presentation. Guard reactions, including movement and retaliation, use combat authority and seeded rolls there. The controller resumes for another decision if it can still act, then hands off. Free objectives can run with no AP and may extract the actor or end the mission.
9. **When nothing moves.** A blocked carrier can guard its lane by waiting for actual space from an escort. A soldier can Reload when dry or when one SP and the last AP make topping up useful, Shield when expected prevented damage warrants it, or Guard when a legal reaction lane or plausible one-move approach exists. The low-value-action state suppresses repeated defensive loops. Wait submits a real end-unit-action request. A Wait from an impossible corridor or a cautious hold is expected behavior, and diagnostics may distinguish that from a service or query blockage.

## Useful distinctions

- **Legality vs preference.** Terrain and live occupancy can prevent a move. A squad promise can veto that move in the planner. Poor exposure can make it lose to holding. Each has a different owner.
- **Proposal vs receipt.** A move preview, rescue, terminal or extraction choice can be recorded before submission. Several combat branches explain the choice after the action window, and terminal battle teardown can lose that explanation even when the action and replay receipt are valid.
- **Focus.** Squad target count discourages piling onto the same target, and the penalty fades for likely finishes. It does not enforce a fire order.
- **Reproducibility.** The actor's difficulty RNG uses the configured decision seed and the unit-name hash. The service owns a separate seeded combat stream. Planner timing controls where scoring yields, not which candidates are sampled on an unchanged board. An actual state change across the yield can invalidate work. The simulator compares repeated choices. Replay verifies saved transactions, actual results and authoritative fingerprints without choosing targets again.
