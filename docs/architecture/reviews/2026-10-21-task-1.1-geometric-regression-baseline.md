## Permanent coverage

Two focused suites now protect the three reported geometry contracts:

- **F1 — road openings:** the test instantiates each actual road GLB, traverses its imported triangle surfaces with resolved node transforms, samples the raised top pavement along all four physical boundaries, and derives openings from bounded gaps. It then presents the corresponding module through `ModularEnvironmentPresenter` and compares those physical world-space openings with independently quarter-turned authored connector directions. It covers straight, T, cross, and curve modules at every authored rotation. Unsupported rotations are excluded using each definition's authored `allowed_rotations` rather than being reported as geometry failures.
- **F2 — module yaw:** the test presents the asymmetric `city_kit_industrial/building-i` shell through the real presentation path and transforms its reviewed model-local working face. Expected world frontages for rotations 0–3 are an explicit test oracle (`+Z`, `-X`, `-Z`, `+X`); the test does not invoke the production yaw or direction-rotation helper to calculate them.
- **F3 — visual envelopes:** `tests/coherent_visual_envelope_test.gd` uses square, offset 2×5, and offset 4×3 modules. A test-side eight-corner transform computes expected bounds for all four rotations using explicit quarter-turn cases, then applies the documented 0.01 seam shrink. Placement cases also detect missed intersections and false rejections while proving that the visual-only fixture has no tactical footprint.

The road test uses the AABB only to locate the mesh boundary and its top elevation. Opening direction comes from triangle coverage rather than AABB proportions, metadata, asset names, or a copied list of expected mesh directions.

## Per-defect sensitivity evidence

### F1 — imported road geometry versus connectors

- `road_straight` failed rotations 0, 1, 2, and 3. At rotation 0, connectors expected openings on `-Z/+Z`; the physical mesh exposed `-X/+X`.
- `road_intersection_t` failed rotations 1 and 3. Its asymmetrical third opening was mirrored by the old positive-yaw convention. Rotations 0 and 2 happened to preserve the same unordered opening set.
- `road_crossroad` passed every rotation because its four-way opening set is rotationally symmetric. The test still verifies that all four physical openings exist and correspond to all four connectors; this asset cannot independently reveal a quarter-turn sign error.
- `road_corner` failed rotations 0 and 2. At rotation 0, connectors expected `-Z/+X`; physical openings were `-X/+Z`. Rotations 1 and 3 happened to produce the same unordered set under the combination of the old curve normalization and yaw reversal.

These results reproduce F1 using actual imported surface topology and resolved presentation transforms.

### F2 — logical rotation versus rendered yaw

`building-i` passed rotations 0 and 2 and failed rotations 1 and 3:

- Rotation 1 expected `-X`; rendered working face was `+X`.
- Rotation 3 expected `+X`; rendered working face was `-X`.

This exactly reproduces the assessment's quarter-turn reversal through the actual asymmetric building presentation path.

### F3 — rectangular envelope rotation

The offset 2×5 probe passed rotations 0 and 1 and failed rotations 2 and 3:

- Rotation 2 expected position `(9.76, 3.11, 19.76)` and got `(12.76, 3.11, 19.76)`; size agreed at `(1.48, 1.98, 3.73)`.
- Rotation 3 expected position `(10.51, 3.11, 19.76)` and got `(10.51, 3.11, 22.76)`; size agreed at `(3.73, 1.98, 1.48)`.

This reproduces the incorrect positional reservation while demonstrating that tactical footprint data is not involved.

## Limitations

- Road openings are inferred from bounded gaps in the calibrated road kit's highest horizontal pavement surface. This is objective for the four supported road assets, but a future pack with bridges, multi-level road surfaces, or no raised border may require a different geometry probe or explicit verified sockets.
- The crossroad's rotational symmetry means it validates opening completeness and connector compatibility, but cannot diagnose yaw sign by itself. The asymmetric T, curve, and building tests provide that sensitivity.
- The presentation checks validate instantiated transforms and real imported mesh topology headlessly. They do not perform pixel comparison or replace the separate rendered visual-inspection workflow.
- Only rotations declared by a module are contractual. An asset/module pair without an authored definition is unsupported rather than a failed road configuration.
